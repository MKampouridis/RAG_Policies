A PGR student sponsored on a Student visa wants to intermit for two terms. What are the consequences?

Reference answer

The University is required to withdraw sponsorship from a student who intermits, and the Home Office will curtail their immigration permission. The student will have to leave the UK while intermitting, and must stop any work immediately because the condition allowing work alongside study no longer applies. Before returning they can request further immigration sponsorship and, if eligible, apply for a new Student visa. Retrospective intermission is not possible for sponsored students.

The assistant's answer

If a PGR student sponsored on a Student visa intermits for two terms, the key consequences are: - **Loss of sponsorship**: The University must withdraw sponsorship, since it can only sponsor a student for a Student visa while they are fully registered and actively engaged in study. - **Visa curtailment**: The Home Office will curtail (cut short) the student's immigration permission once the University reports the intermission. - **Must leave the UK**: The student will have to leave the UK for the duration of the intermission period. - **Must stop working**: If the student has been working under the conditions of their visa, they must stop immediately, since the work permission tied to being a registered student no longer applies. - **No retrospective intermission**: This route is not available to sponsored students, so the request and its immigration consequences must be handled prospectively, not after the fact. - **Re-entry process**: Before returning to study in the UK, the student would need to request further immigration sponsorship from the University and, if eligible, apply for a new Student visa to re-enter. - **Notification**: When the University reports the change to the Home Office, its International Services Team will notify the student by email and provide immigration advice. Note that a two-term intermission for non-medical reasons is within the permitted maximum (up to 3 terms for non-medical intermission before a student would be required to withdraw and seek readmission later), so the length itself would not automatically trigger withdrawal from the programme — but the visa consequences above would still apply regardless of term length, since any intermission (not just a short, unsponsored absence) triggers the loss of sponsorship. Source: https://www.essex.ac.uk/-/media/documents/directories/academic-section/pgre/pgr-intermission-absence.pdf

Are there membership rules for that RSPB?

Reference answer

Yes. Where the RSPB is considering a recommendation of discontinuation or downgrading, or a change of status to MPhil at confirmation, its membership must include at least two members who were not on the student's last Supervisory Panel. Smaller departments may co-opt a supervisor from another department in a cognate discipline.

The assistant's answer

Yes. The Code of Practice for Postgraduate Research Degrees sets out membership rules for the RSPB: - The RSPB is established by each department and chaired by the **Postgraduate Research Director or Head of Department**. - It must include **at least two other experienced supervisors**. - The Chair must **not** be a partner or close relative of the student's supervisor (main, joint or secondary), nor have any professional or personal relationship with the student that could create a conflict of interest. - Where the RSPB is considering a **recommendation of discontinuation or downgrading**, or a **confirmation decision to change a student's status to MPhil**, the RSPB membership must include **at least two members who were not on the student's last Supervisory Panel**. - Smaller departments that struggle to meet this requirement may **co-opt a supervisor from another department** in a cognate discipline to serve on the RSPB. This applies generally across departments — no separate rules specific to CSEE or History are given.
