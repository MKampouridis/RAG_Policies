University of Essex Landing page drafts

Five ways to reconcile the two UIs

All five keep the production purple bar and lime keyline verbatim, use Archivo and the Essex palette, and follow the system theme. They differ in how much guidance sits on the landing page — which is the actual tension between the original's calm and the new UI's helpfulness. Static drafts: nothing is wired up.

1 · Bare

Purple bar, headline, composer, and four example questions as a plain underlined list. Closest to the original's restraint; the examples read as a quiet menu rather than a feature.

Most calm · least visual pull toward the examples

2 · Cards

Centred hero with the four examples as a 2×2 grid of bordered cards. The new UI's structure, stripped of its corner marks and grey ground.

Most discoverable · busiest landing

3 · In place

No separate landing screen at all. The sidebar and composer are always present; the empty conversation area holds the welcome and four short topic chips. Nothing to navigate away from.

No screen switching · least room for explanation

4 · Purple hero

The purple bar grows into a full hero band carrying the headline, with the composer lifted to straddle its lower edge. The most Essex-looking of the five.

Strongest brand presence · heaviest top

5 · Progressive

Headline and composer only. Examples and "how it works" are collapsed behind disclosures — open on first visit, closable for good.

Calm that still teaches · one extra click
