**Answer now:**

Yes, but the detail is limited. According to the PGR intermission/absence guidance:

- **Full-time to part-time**: If a student wants to reduce their commitment but hasn't yet completed their minimum period of registration, they may have the option to shift from full-time to part-time status — rather than take a leave of absence. ## 13. why would an independent chair be required to oversee the conduct of the oral examination?

<details><summary>Answer then</summary>

```
The Faculty Dean (Postgraduate) may appoint an Independent Chair to oversee the conduct of the oral examination. [https://www.essex.ac.uk/-/media/documents/about/governance/code-practice-vivas.pdf] Additionally, they are required when a staff candidate is being examined. [https://www.essex.ac.uk/-/media/documents/about/governance/code-practice-vivas.pdf]
```

</details>

**Answer now:**

The material I can see explains what an Independent Chair *does* once appointed, rather than giving a rationale statement for *why* the role exists, but the responsibilities listed point to the underlying purpose:

- **To oversee procedural compliance** – ensuring the examination is conducted in accordance with the University's procedures for research degree vivas.
- **To brief the examiners on process** – attending the pre-viva meeting to advise on procedural matters and help the examiners agree their approach, without having read the thesis themselves.
- **To manage the start of the viva** – introducing the examiners and explaining the format to the candidate.
- **To mediate between examiners** – where necessary, helping identify possible recommendations and facilitate an agreed outcome if the examiners' views diverge.
- **To safeguard the candidate's opportunity to perform** – ensuring the conduct of the viva allows the candidate to demonstrate their knowledge and explain/defend their work.
- **To sign off on the process** – confirming in the Joint Report Form that the viva was conducted with an Independent Chair present and that they are satisfied with its conduct.

Taken together, these responsibilities suggest the role exists to provide independent procedural oversight and impartiality in situations that carry higher risk of unfairness or inconsistency — for example, where an internal examiner lacks experience, where a thesis has been referred and is being re-examined, following an appeal, where the candidate is a staff member, or where other circumstances affecting the candidate warrant extra safeguards (as covered in the earlier list of triggering circumstances). The Independent Chair essentially acts as a neutral, non-assessing party to guarantee due process and fairness rather than to judge the academic content of the thesis.
